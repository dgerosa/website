This problem is actually more general than gravitational-wave astronomy (but we apply it to GWs here, sorry), namely that of sources that evolve while being observed. If their properties are quoted at some reference epoch (time or frequency, or any other signpost), then inferring their collective properties becomes challenging (or at least the interpretation becomes challenging), because I'm left with an inferred population of sources made up of objects at different stages of their evolution. For gravitational-wave astronomy (here we go), this is the case for both spins and eccentricity, which evolve. [Spins are kind of OK](https://davidegerosa.com/posts/2021-10-13-gravitational-wave-population-inference-at-past-time-infinity) because they admit a well-defined limit at past infinity, i.e., when propagated back in time. This paper looks at the eccentricity problem, which is more complicated because, if propagated naively, all binaries tend to have $e=1$ at large separations. We propose an (IMO elegant) solution, which is to use the Newtonian angular momentum to parametrize the dynamics instead of eccentricity, and things seem to work.




C. Anselmo, G. Fumagalli, **D. Gerosa**.\
[arXiv:2611.15738 [gr-qc]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15738).
